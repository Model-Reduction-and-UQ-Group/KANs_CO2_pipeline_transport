# Impact of Impurities on Carbon Dioxide Transport in Pipelines

published in Applied Energy xxx (2026) xxxxx, https://doi.org/

Large-scale deployment of carbon capture and storage (CCS) technologies involves safe and efficient transport of CO2 in pressurized pipelines, from industrial facilities to storage sites. Captured CO2 streams typically contain variable amounts of impurities such as N2, O2, CH4, and H2, which can significantly affect  thermophysical properties of the CO2 mixture, altering its phase behavior and pressure and temperature evolution along the pipeline. We investigate the influence of compositional variability of such mixtures on CO2 transport using Kolmogorov-Arnold networks (KANs). The latter are trained and validated on high-fidelity simulations of CO2 mixtures under varying inlet conditions and pipe geometries. Our numerical experiments demonstrate that KAN surrogates accurately reproduce the pressure distribution along a pipeline, including the onset of two-phase conditions across a wide range of operating and compositional scenarios. A sensitivity analysis reveals the relative impact of individual impurities and operational parameters on the transport behavior. Our framework provides a tool for uncertainty quantification and design optimization of CO2 transport systems under realistic conditions, supporting the reliable scaling of CCS infrastructure.
